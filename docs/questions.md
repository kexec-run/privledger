# Recording Guide

## Why this exists

When I first started using the pipeline, I often had nothing to say.

Blank mind. No structure. Just me staring at the camera.

So I created this — a minimal set of questions to build consistency, reduce friction, and give the recording a basic shape.

This is not a script. It's a fallback system for when thinking fails.

This structure loosely maps to metadata fields (mood, tags, focus, location).

---

## Core Principle

- Start and end matter most
- Middle is structure, not obligation
- Questions are checkpoints, not a checklist
- Speak naturally — ignore all questions if you're already talking
- If you're speaking naturally, stay with it — don't interrupt yourself to answer questions

---

## Opening

Always start here.

1. Date and time
2. Why this recording? (routine / specific event)
3. Location (room → city)
4. What was I doing in the last ~2 hours?
5. Mood (one word)

This should take under 30 seconds.

---

## Middle (optional)

Use only what applies. Do not force all of them.

**Activity & Attention**
- What consumed most of my attention?

**Events**
- Did anything unexpected happen?

**Emotional signals**
- Did anything frustrate me?
- Did anything excite or interest me?

---

## Reflection

- Did the day go as expected?
- What felt wasted?
- What felt useful?

---

## Status

Quick snapshot. Do not over-explain.

- What progress did I make?
- Did I move closer to my current goals?
- What did I plan but couldn't do?

**Friction & habits**
- Where did things break down?
- Any bad habits (e.g. procrastination, smoking)?

If this becomes long, you're overthinking it.

---

## Forward Look

Close here.

- What will I likely do after this?
- What is the main thing for tomorrow?

---

## Mindset

Treat this as speaking to your future self — or a person you will meet later.

Not a performance. Not analysis. Just an honest record.

This is not an interview. There are no score points.

Speak naturally. Stay with your flow.

Some days you'll have a lot to say. Some days, almost nothing. Both are valid.

- If you notice you're judging what you're saying, say that you're judging it
- It's okay to say very little

**Consistency > content.**
